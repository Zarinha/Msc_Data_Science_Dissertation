## 🧭 Project Overview  

This dissertation investigates the **technical, economic, and environmental viability** of producing and exporting **sugarcane-based ethanol** from **Mozambique, South Africa, and Kenya** to **Europe**, using **Brazil’s export model to Sweden** as a comparative reference.  

The research integrates **predictive modeling**, **comparative analysis**, and **life-cycle assessment (LCA)** to determine whether African ethanol can viably support Europe’s renewable fuel targets under **EU RED II 2030** sustainability criteria.  

---

## 🧠 Research Question  

> **What is the viability of sugarcane ethanol production and export from Mozambique, South Africa, and Kenya to Europe, compared to Brazil’s export model to Sweden?**

---

## 🧩 Hypotheses  

| ID | Hypothesis | Focus |
|----|-------------|--------|
| **H1** | Sugarcane production in Mozambique, South Africa, and Kenya is climatically and agronomically viable. | Production modeling |
| **H2** | Export economics are competitive with Brazil’s ethanol model under similar logistic and trade conditions. | Economic modeling |
| **H3** | Sugarcane ethanol meets or exceeds EU 2030 RED II carbon reduction thresholds. | Life Cycle Analysis |
| **H4** | Ethanol remains a viable decarbonization alternative compared to electric vehicles (EVs) given abundant alcohol availability. | Scenario analysis |
| **H5** | Large-scale adoption of ethanol-fueled vehicles in Europe depends on access to Brazilian/Swedish flex-fuel technology. | Policy & technology analysis |

## ⚙️ Methodological Framework  

| Hypothesis | Method / Model | Tool | Output |

| **H1** | Regression / Crop yield prediction | Python (Pandas, Scikit-Learn) | Predicted yield & production cost |
